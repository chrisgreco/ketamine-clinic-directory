## How Much Does Ketamine Therapy Cost in 2026?

A single ketamine IV infusion costs $400 to $800 in the United States. An initial series of six infusions, which is the most common starting protocol, typically runs $2,400 to $4,800. But the total cost of ketamine therapy depends on the type of treatment, your location, the clinic you choose, and whether insurance covers any portion of the expense.

If you are considering ketamine therapy for depression, PTSD, chronic pain, or another condition, understanding the full cost picture is essential for making an informed decision. This guide breaks down pricing by treatment type, explains insurance realities, and offers practical strategies for managing the financial side of treatment.

**Important:** The pricing information below is based on reported averages across U.S. clinics as of early 2026. Actual costs at your local provider may differ. Always confirm pricing directly with your chosen clinic before beginning treatment, and consult your physician to discuss whether ketamine therapy may be appropriate for your situation.

### IV Ketamine Infusion

IV infusion is the most widely studied and commonly offered form of ketamine therapy. The higher cost reflects the medical supervision required: an IV line, continuous vital sign monitoring, and post-infusion observation. Infusions typically last 40 to 60 minutes, with an additional 30 to 60 minutes of recovery time.

Most clinics price IV infusions between $400 and $800 per session. Metro areas like New York, Los Angeles, and San Francisco tend to be on the higher end. Clinics in smaller markets may charge closer to the $400 mark. Some providers offer package pricing for the standard six-infusion series.

### IM (Intramuscular) Injection

Intramuscular ketamine injections are less common than IV but offer a lower price point, typically $250 to $500 per session. The onset is slightly slower than IV, and the experience may differ somewhat in intensity. Some clinics prefer IM for certain patient populations or as part of ketamine-assisted psychotherapy (KAP) protocols.

### Intranasal Ketamine (Compounded)

Compounded intranasal ketamine (not to be confused with FDA-approved Spravato) is prescribed by some providers for at-home use between clinic visits. Costs range from $100 to $300 per treatment cycle. This form requires a prescription and is prepared by a compounding pharmacy.

### Spravato (Esketamine)

Spravato is the brand name for FDA-approved esketamine nasal spray. Because it has FDA approval for treatment-resistant depression, it is more likely to be covered by insurance than other ketamine formulations. However, it must be administered in a certified clinic under the REMS program, and patients must remain under observation for at least two hours.

Out-of-pocket costs for Spravato range from $600 to $900 per session. With insurance, copays may be significantly lower, sometimes $10 to $150 per session depending on your plan. Janssen (the manufacturer) also offers a patient savings program for eligible patients.

### Sublingual and Oral Ketamine

Some providers prescribe sublingual (under the tongue) or oral ketamine tablets, often as maintenance therapy following an initial IV or IM series. These typically cost $100 to $250 per treatment cycle and are sometimes prescribed for at-home use with telehealth supervision.

## Does Insurance Cover Ketamine Therapy?

The short answer: it depends on the type of ketamine.

**Spravato (esketamine)** is FDA-approved and has established insurance billing codes. Many commercial insurance plans, Medicare, and some Medicaid programs cover Spravato, though prior authorization is usually required and coverage varies by plan. Patients should expect to meet their deductible first.

**IV and IM racemic ketamine** are used off-label and are rarely covered by commercial insurance. Most patients pay out of pocket. However, some clinics have found ways to bill certain components of the visit (such as the monitoring fee or psychiatric evaluation) through insurance, reducing the total out-of-pocket cost.

**What to ask your insurance company:**

- Does my plan cover Spravato (esketamine) for treatment-resistant depression?
- What is my copay or coinsurance for Spravato?
- Is prior authorization required?
- Does my plan cover the psychiatric evaluation or monitoring components of off-label ketamine treatment?

## How to Budget for Ketamine Therapy

The initial series of six infusions is the largest expense. After that, maintenance sessions are typically needed every four to eight weeks, costing $400 to $800 each. Annual maintenance costs often range from $2,400 to $9,600 depending on frequency.

**Strategies to manage costs:**

- **Ask about package pricing.** Many clinics offer discounts when you prepay for a series of six infusions, sometimes saving 10 to 20 percent.
- **Explore financing options.** Some clinics accept CareCredit, Prosper Healthcare Lending, or similar medical financing services that offer interest-free payment plans.
- **Consider treatment type.** If IV infusion is beyond your budget, discuss IM injection or sublingual options with your provider, which may offer cost savings.
- **Check for Spravato coverage.** If you have insurance, Spravato may be partially or fully covered, making it more affordable than out-of-pocket IV treatment.
- **Use HSA or FSA funds.** Ketamine therapy is generally eligible for payment through health savings accounts (HSA) and flexible spending accounts (FSA).

## What Is Included in the Session Price?

Reputable clinics should include the following in their per-session price:

- Pre-treatment vital signs and medical assessment
- The ketamine medication itself
- IV line setup and removal (for IV infusions)
- Continuous monitoring during the infusion (blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation)
- Post-infusion observation period
- Clinical staff oversight throughout the session

Some clinics charge separately for the initial psychiatric evaluation, which typically costs $200 to $400. Ask your provider for a complete cost breakdown before starting treatment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ketamine therapy worth the cost?

The value of ketamine therapy depends on your individual response and the severity of your condition. For patients with treatment-resistant depression or severe chronic pain who have not responded to conventional treatments, ketamine may provide meaningful relief. Studies suggest that approximately 50 to 70 percent of patients with treatment-resistant depression experience improvement with ketamine, though results vary. Discuss the potential benefits and costs with your physician to determine if it may be appropriate for your situation.

### Can I use my HSA or FSA to pay for ketamine therapy?

In most cases, yes. Ketamine therapy prescribed by a licensed medical provider is generally considered a qualified medical expense under HSA and FSA guidelines. Keep your receipts and consult your plan administrator if you have questions.

### Why is ketamine therapy so expensive?

The cost reflects the medical supervision required for safe administration. IV ketamine infusions require a clinical setting with monitoring equipment, trained medical staff, and post-infusion observation. The medication cost itself is relatively low; the majority of the price covers the clinical infrastructure and professional oversight.

### Are there low-cost ketamine therapy options?

Some research institutions and academic medical centers offer ketamine therapy at reduced rates, particularly for patients who qualify for clinical trials. At-home sublingual ketamine programs may also be more affordable than in-clinic IV infusions, though the evidence base for at-home protocols is more limited. Always prioritize safety and proper medical supervision over cost savings.

### How many sessions will I need?

Most protocols start with an initial series of six infusions over two to three weeks. After that, maintenance sessions are typically recommended every four to eight weeks. Some patients require more frequent maintenance, while others may extend intervals over time. Your provider will develop a personalized treatment plan based on your response.

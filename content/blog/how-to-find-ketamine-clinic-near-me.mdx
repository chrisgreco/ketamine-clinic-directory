## How to Find a Legitimate Ketamine Clinic Near You

The number of ketamine clinics in the United States has grown significantly in recent years, and the quality of providers varies widely. Some clinics are operated by board-certified psychiatrists and anesthesiologists with rigorous safety protocols. Others may cut corners on monitoring, staffing, or patient screening.

Finding the right clinic is one of the most important decisions you will make in your ketamine therapy journey. This guide provides seven specific questions to ask any potential provider, along with explanations of why each question matters and what constitutes an acceptable answer.

**Important:** This guide is for educational purposes only. It does not constitute medical advice or an endorsement of any specific provider. Always consult your physician before beginning ketamine therapy.

## 7 Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Clinic

### 1. What are the medical credentials of the provider administering my treatment?

**Why it matters:** Ketamine is a powerful medication that requires proper medical oversight. The provider overseeing your treatment should have relevant training and board certification.

**What to look for:** Board-certified anesthesiologists, psychiatrists, emergency medicine physicians, or pain management specialists are the most common providers of ketamine therapy. Nurse practitioners and physician assistants may administer treatment under physician supervision. Ask specifically about their training in ketamine therapy and how long they have been offering it.

**Red flag:** If the clinic cannot clearly identify the medical director or the credentials of the person overseeing your infusion, look elsewhere.

### 2. What monitoring equipment and safety protocols do you have in place?

**Why it matters:** Ketamine affects blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen levels. Continuous monitoring is essential for safe administration.

**What to look for:** At minimum, the clinic should use pulse oximetry (oxygen monitoring), blood pressure monitoring (automated, at regular intervals), and cardiac monitoring during every infusion. Staff trained in Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) should be present. Emergency equipment including supplemental oxygen, suction, and emergency medications should be immediately available.

**Red flag:** If a clinic does not monitor vital signs continuously during the infusion or lacks emergency equipment, this is a serious safety concern.

### 3. How do you screen patients before treatment?

**Why it matters:** Not everyone is a candidate for ketamine therapy. Proper screening helps identify patients who may be at higher risk for adverse effects.

**What to look for:** A thorough initial evaluation should include a psychiatric history review, current medication assessment, medical history (particularly cardiovascular and liver conditions), substance use screening, and pregnancy testing when appropriate. The clinic should have clear contraindication criteria and should not administer ketamine to patients who do not meet safety requirements.

**Red flag:** If a clinic schedules your first infusion without a comprehensive evaluation, or if they seem willing to treat anyone regardless of history, reconsider.

### 4. What is your treatment protocol, and how do you measure outcomes?

**Why it matters:** Effective ketamine therapy follows evidence-based protocols and includes systematic outcome tracking.

**What to look for:** Most clinics follow a protocol of six infusions over two to three weeks for the initial series, with maintenance sessions as needed. The provider should use standardized assessment tools (such as the PHQ-9 for depression or the PCL-5 for PTSD) to measure your symptoms before, during, and after treatment. Ask how they determine whether the treatment is working and what happens if you do not respond.

**Red flag:** Clinics that offer a one-size-fits-all approach without measuring outcomes or adjusting protocols based on individual response may not provide optimal care.

### 5. Do you coordinate with my existing treatment team?

**Why it matters:** Ketamine therapy works best as part of a comprehensive treatment plan. Your ketamine provider should communicate with your psychiatrist, therapist, and primary care physician.

**What to look for:** The clinic should request permission to contact your existing providers, send treatment summaries, and coordinate medication management. If you are also receiving psychotherapy, the ketamine provider should be aware of your therapy goals and timeline.

**Red flag:** A clinic that operates in isolation without communicating with your other providers is not providing integrated care.

### 6. What is your complete pricing structure?

**Why it matters:** The total cost of ketamine therapy extends beyond the per-session price. Hidden fees can add up significantly.

**What to look for:** Ask for a complete cost breakdown including the initial evaluation fee, per-session pricing, any separate monitoring or medication fees, package discounts, and maintenance session pricing. Confirm whether the quoted price is all-inclusive or if there are additional charges. If you have insurance, ask whether the clinic can bill any components of the treatment.

**Red flag:** Vague pricing, reluctance to provide written cost estimates, or unexpected charges after treatment begins.

### 7. What is your approach to patient safety and follow-up?

**Why it matters:** Responsible ketamine providers have clear protocols for managing side effects, handling emergencies, and following up between sessions.

**What to look for:** The clinic should provide clear written instructions for before and after each session, have a system for contacting a provider between appointments if needed, and schedule regular check-ins to assess your progress. Ask what happens if you experience adverse effects at home after a session.

**Red flag:** If there is no after-hours contact option or if the clinic does not follow up between sessions, the level of patient care may be insufficient.

## Additional Tips for Finding a Clinic

- **Check state licensing boards** to verify the provider's medical license is active and in good standing.
- **Look for reviews from multiple sources** including Google, Healthgrades, and patient forums. No provider should have exclusively five-star reviews.
- **Schedule a consultation** before committing. Most reputable clinics offer an initial consultation (sometimes at no cost) to determine if you are a candidate.
- **Trust your instincts.** If something feels rushed, unprofessional, or overly sales-oriented, consider other options.
- **Ask about their experience volume.** Clinics that have treated hundreds or thousands of patients generally have more refined protocols and experience managing complications.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many ketamine clinics are there in the United States?

Estimates suggest there are over 800 ketamine clinics operating in the U.S. as of 2026. The number has grown rapidly, which makes careful evaluation even more important. Our directory lists verified providers to help you start your search.

### Should I choose a ketamine-only clinic or a multi-service psychiatric practice?

Both models can provide excellent care. Ketamine-only clinics may have more specialized experience and higher treatment volumes. Multi-service practices may offer better integration with ongoing psychiatric care. The most important factors are the provider's credentials, safety protocols, and outcomes tracking.

### Is it safe to receive ketamine therapy at a med spa or wellness center?

Exercise caution with non-medical settings. While some wellness-oriented practices provide safe ketamine therapy, the medical oversight, monitoring equipment, and emergency preparedness may not meet the same standards as a medical clinic. Always verify that a licensed physician oversees the treatment and that continuous vital sign monitoring is in place.

### How far should I be willing to travel for a ketamine clinic?

For many patients, the nearest quality provider is the best choice given the frequency of the initial treatment series (two to three sessions per week). However, if the closest clinic raises quality concerns based on the questions above, traveling further for a reputable provider is generally worth the investment.

### What if I cannot find a ketamine clinic near me?

Some providers offer telehealth consultations combined with at-home sublingual ketamine protocols for patients in underserved areas. While this approach has a more limited evidence base than in-clinic IV treatment, it may be an option when geographic access is a barrier. Discuss this with your physician.

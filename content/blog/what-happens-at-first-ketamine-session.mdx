## What Happens at Your First Ketamine Therapy Session?

Your first ketamine therapy session can feel uncertain, especially if you are unsure what to expect. This step-by-step guide walks through the entire process, from arriving at the clinic to heading home after your infusion.

Every clinic operates slightly differently, but the core elements of a safe ketamine session are consistent. Understanding the process in advance can help reduce anxiety and allow you to focus on the treatment itself.

**Important:** This is a general guide based on common clinic practices. Your specific experience may differ depending on the clinic, your treatment protocol, and your medical history. Always follow the instructions provided by your specific provider, and consult your physician with any questions about whether ketamine therapy is appropriate for you.

## Before You Arrive: Preparation

### What to Do

- **Fast for 4 to 6 hours.** Most clinics require you to avoid eating for at least four hours before your infusion to reduce the risk of nausea. Clear liquids are usually permitted up to two hours before.
- **Arrange transportation.** You will not be able to drive after your session. Have someone drive you, or arrange a rideshare service. Plan to have transportation available for the rest of the day.
- **Wear comfortable clothing.** You will be reclined in a chair or bed for about two hours total. Loose-fitting clothing with easy IV access on your arm is ideal.
- **Bring your ID and insurance card** (if applicable), along with any intake forms you were asked to complete in advance.
- **Bring a list of all current medications** and supplements, including dosages.

### What to Avoid

- **Do not drive yourself.** This is non-negotiable. The dissociative effects of ketamine impair driving ability for several hours.
- **Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours** before your session.
- **Do not take benzodiazepines** the morning of your session unless specifically instructed by your provider. Benzodiazepines may reduce the effectiveness of ketamine in some patients.
- **Avoid caffeine** on the day of treatment, as it may increase anxiety or blood pressure.
- **Do not make important decisions** or plan demanding activities for the rest of the day.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1: Arrival and Check-In (10-15 minutes)

When you arrive at the clinic, you will check in at the front desk and complete any remaining intake paperwork. If this is your very first visit, you may have a brief consultation with the treating physician or nurse practitioner to review your medical history, current medications, and treatment goals.

Some clinics combine the initial evaluation and first infusion into a single appointment, while others require a separate evaluation visit beforehand.

### Step 2: Pre-Infusion Assessment (10 minutes)

A nurse or medical assistant will take your baseline vital signs, including blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature. These readings provide a reference point for monitoring during the infusion.

You will be shown to your treatment room or chair. Most clinics provide a private or semi-private space with a reclining chair, blankets, and an option for eye shades or headphones. Some clinics offer curated music playlists designed for the ketamine experience.

### Step 3: IV Placement

A nurse will place a small IV catheter, typically in your hand or forearm. This is a brief pinch similar to a blood draw. The IV line allows for precise delivery of the ketamine medication and the ability to adjust the dose during the infusion.

If you are receiving intramuscular (IM) ketamine or intranasal treatment, the process differs, but the monitoring and observation steps remain similar.

### Step 4: The Infusion (40-60 minutes)

The ketamine infusion begins. The medication is delivered slowly through the IV over approximately 40 to 60 minutes. Here is what you might experience as the infusion progresses:

**First 5 to 10 minutes:** You may notice a slight warmth or tingling sensation. Some patients feel a subtle shift in their awareness or a sense of relaxation.

**10 to 30 minutes:** The dissociative effects typically become more noticeable. You may experience:

- A floating or weightless feeling
- Visual changes such as colors appearing more vivid or objects appearing to shift
- Altered perception of time (minutes may feel longer or shorter)
- Emotional experiences ranging from peaceful calm to deep introspection
- A sense of detachment from your body or surroundings

**30 to 60 minutes:** The experience generally reaches its peak intensity and then begins to taper. Your medical team monitors your vital signs throughout and can adjust the infusion rate if needed.

**Throughout the infusion:** Medical staff monitor your blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen levels continuously. If you experience significant nausea, they can administer anti-nausea medication through the IV. If dissociation becomes uncomfortable, the rate can be slowed or paused.

Many patients keep their eyes closed and listen to music during the infusion. The clinical team is available if you need anything.

### Step 5: Post-Infusion Recovery (30-60 minutes)

After the infusion ends, the IV is removed and you remain in the recovery area while the effects gradually subside. Most patients feel noticeably more clear-headed within 15 to 30 minutes of the infusion ending.

During recovery you may feel:

- Mild grogginess or drowsiness
- Some residual floating or lightness
- Emotional tenderness or reflectiveness
- Mild unsteadiness when you first stand

The clinical team will check your vital signs again and ensure you are stable before discharge. They will confirm that your transportation is arranged and provide any written post-session instructions.

### Step 6: Going Home

Your driver will take you home. For the rest of the day:

- Do not drive, operate machinery, or use power tools
- Avoid alcohol
- Stay hydrated and eat a light meal when you feel ready
- Avoid making major decisions
- Rest if you feel tired
- Take note of any changes in your mood, pain levels, or thought patterns

Some patients feel a noticeable mood lift or pain reduction within hours of their first session. Others do not notice changes until after two or three sessions. Both responses are normal.

## What to Bring

- Government-issued ID
- Insurance card (if applicable)
- Complete medication and supplement list
- Comfortable clothing
- Eye mask or sunglasses (some clinics provide these)
- Headphones if you prefer your own music
- A journal (optional, for post-session reflections)
- A trusted person to drive you home

## Common Concerns

**Will I be unconscious?** No. Therapeutic doses of ketamine are sub-anesthetic, meaning you remain conscious throughout. You may feel detached from your surroundings, but you will be aware and able to communicate if needed.

**What if I feel scared during the infusion?** Anxiety during the experience is not uncommon, especially during the first session. Let your medical team know if you are feeling uncomfortable. They can slow or pause the infusion and provide reassurance. Many patients find that subsequent sessions are easier as they know what to expect.

**Will I remember the experience?** Most patients remember the general experience, though details may be hazy. Some patients find the experience meaningful or insightful, while others simply find it unusual. Neither response predicts treatment effectiveness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does the entire first appointment take?

Plan for approximately two to three hours for your first session, including check-in, pre-infusion assessment, the infusion itself (40 to 60 minutes), and post-infusion recovery (30 to 60 minutes). Subsequent sessions may be slightly shorter since the check-in process is faster.

### Will I feel the effects of ketamine immediately after my first session?

Some patients notice mood improvement or pain reduction within hours of their first infusion. Others do not experience significant changes until after several sessions. Both patterns are normal. Your provider will evaluate your response over the course of the initial treatment series.

### Can I eat after my session?

Yes. Once the effects have worn off and you feel ready, you can eat normally. Many patients prefer to start with a light meal. Staying hydrated is also recommended.

### What if I need to use the restroom during the infusion?

While possible, most clinics recommend using the restroom before the infusion begins. If needed during the session, a staff member can assist you. The IV can be temporarily paused.

### Should I bring someone into the treatment room with me?

Policies vary by clinic. Some allow a support person in the room, while others prefer that patients are alone to minimize distractions. Ask your clinic about their policy when scheduling your appointment.

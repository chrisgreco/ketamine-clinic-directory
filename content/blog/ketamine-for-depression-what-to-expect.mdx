## Ketamine for Depression: What to Expect

If you have been living with depression that has not responded to traditional antidepressants, your physician may have mentioned ketamine therapy as a potential option. Ketamine has emerged as one of the most significant developments in depression treatment in decades, with studies suggesting it can produce rapid-acting antidepressant effects in patients who have not responded to conventional medications.

This guide explains how ketamine works for depression, what a typical treatment timeline looks like, and what you might experience during and after a session. As with any medical treatment, consult your physician to determine if ketamine therapy is appropriate for your specific situation.

**Important:** This article provides general educational information and is not medical advice. Individual responses to ketamine therapy vary significantly. Always work with a qualified healthcare provider to make treatment decisions.

## How Ketamine Works for Depression: The NMDA Mechanism

Traditional antidepressants like SSRIs and SNRIs target the serotonin and norepinephrine systems. Ketamine works through an entirely different mechanism, which is one reason it may help patients who have not responded to those medications.

Ketamine primarily acts as an NMDA (N-methyl-D-aspartate) receptor antagonist. By temporarily blocking NMDA receptors, ketamine triggers a cascade of neurobiological events that researchers believe include:

- **Increased BDNF release.** Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a protein that promotes the growth and maintenance of neurons. Studies suggest ketamine rapidly increases BDNF levels, which may help restore neural connections that have been weakened by chronic depression.

- **Enhanced synaptic plasticity.** Research indicates that ketamine promotes the formation of new synaptic connections (synaptogenesis), potentially reversing some of the structural brain changes associated with prolonged depression.

- **Glutamate modulation.** By affecting the glutamate system (the brain's most abundant excitatory neurotransmitter), ketamine appears to activate pathways that produce rapid antidepressant effects, sometimes within hours rather than the weeks required by traditional antidepressants.

- **mTOR pathway activation.** Studies suggest ketamine activates the mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) signaling pathway, which is involved in protein synthesis and the formation of new neural connections.

This mechanism of action is fundamentally different from conventional antidepressants, which is why ketamine may work when other treatments have not. However, researchers are still working to fully understand the complete picture of how ketamine alleviates depression.

## Treatment Timeline: 5 Steps

### Step 1: Initial Consultation and Evaluation

Before receiving ketamine therapy, you will undergo a comprehensive evaluation with the provider. This typically includes a review of your psychiatric history, current medications, medical conditions, and previous treatment attempts. The clinician will assess whether you are a suitable candidate for ketamine therapy.

This evaluation usually takes 30 to 60 minutes and may be conducted in person or via telehealth. Some clinics charge separately for this consultation ($200 to $400).

### Step 2: The Initial Series (Weeks 1-3)

The standard protocol for depression involves six infusions over approximately two to three weeks. Sessions are typically scheduled two to three times per week. This concentrated approach is designed to build upon the effects of each successive infusion.

Many patients report noticing some mood improvement after the first or second infusion, though some do not experience significant changes until later in the series. Your provider will evaluate your response throughout this period.

### Step 3: Response Evaluation

After the initial series, your provider will assess your response to treatment. This evaluation considers symptom reduction, functional improvement, and side effect profile. Approximately 50 to 70 percent of patients with treatment-resistant depression respond to ketamine, according to published studies, though individual results vary.

### Step 4: Maintenance Phase

Patients who respond to the initial series typically transition to maintenance infusions. These are scheduled at gradually increasing intervals, often starting at once every two weeks and potentially extending to once every four to eight weeks depending on your response.

### Step 5: Ongoing Monitoring

Ketamine therapy is most effective as part of a comprehensive treatment plan that may include ongoing psychotherapy, medication management, and lifestyle modifications. Your ketamine provider should coordinate with your existing mental health team.

## What a Ketamine Session Feels Like

Every patient experiences ketamine differently, but here is a general overview of what to expect during an IV infusion for depression:

**Before the infusion:** You will have your vital signs checked (blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen levels). An IV line is placed in your arm or hand. The treatment room is typically quiet and calming.

**During the infusion (40-60 minutes):** As the ketamine takes effect, you may experience:

- A floating or dreamlike sensation
- Mild visual distortions (colors may appear more vivid, shapes may shift)
- A sense of detachment from your body or surroundings (dissociation)
- Altered perception of time
- Emotional experiences ranging from calm to deeply introspective
- Some patients report mild nausea

The intensity varies by dose and individual. Your vital signs are monitored continuously. Medical staff are present throughout and can adjust the infusion rate or provide medication for nausea if needed.

**After the infusion (30-60 minutes of observation):** The dissociative effects typically wear off within 15 to 30 minutes after the infusion ends. You may feel tired, slightly disoriented, or emotionally tender. Some patients describe a sense of clarity or lightness.

You should not drive, operate machinery, or make major decisions for the remainder of the day. Arrange transportation home in advance.

## Contraindications and Precautions

Ketamine therapy may not be appropriate for patients with:

- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Unstable cardiovascular conditions
- Active psychosis or schizophrenia
- Current substance use disorders (particularly stimulants or PCP)
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Certain medication interactions (particularly MAOIs)

This is not a complete list. Disclose your full medical history, all medications (including supplements), and any substance use to your provider during the initial evaluation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How quickly does ketamine work for depression?

Many patients report some improvement in mood within 24 to 72 hours of their first infusion. However, the most robust and sustained effects typically develop over the course of the full initial series of six infusions. Some patients do not respond until later in the treatment course. Consult your provider if you are not experiencing improvement.

### Is ketamine therapy safe for depression?

When administered by qualified medical professionals in a monitored clinical setting, ketamine has a well-established safety profile at sub-anesthetic doses. Side effects are generally mild and temporary. However, like any medical treatment, it carries risks. Discuss the specific risk-benefit profile for your situation with your healthcare provider.

### Can I take my antidepressant while receiving ketamine therapy?

In most cases, yes. Many patients continue their existing antidepressant medications during ketamine therapy. However, certain medications (particularly MAOIs and, in some cases, high-dose benzodiazepines) may need adjustment. Never change your medication regimen without consulting your prescribing physician.

### Will I become addicted to ketamine therapy?

Ketamine has abuse potential and is a Schedule III controlled substance. However, when used in a supervised clinical setting at therapeutic doses, the risk of addiction is considered low by most experts. Your provider should screen for substance use history and monitor for signs of misuse throughout treatment.

### How long do the effects of ketamine therapy last?

The antidepressant effects of a single ketamine infusion typically last days to weeks. With a full initial series and appropriate maintenance sessions, many patients sustain improvement for extended periods. The duration of effect varies significantly between individuals and tends to improve with ongoing treatment.
